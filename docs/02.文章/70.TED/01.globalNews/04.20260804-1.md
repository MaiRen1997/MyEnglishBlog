Canada's **wildfire** season in 2023 was the worst on record.

More than 15 million **hectares** burned, an area larger than England, and we now have a clearer picture of the **consequences** for **climate** change.

 This number is very, very large and very **significant**.

 Researchers found the wildfires in 2023 produced more than 2 billion tons of  greenhouse gas **emissions**. 

That's three times the **emissions** produced by all  other **sectors** in Canada **combined**.

And as it **turns out**, three quarters of those  wildfire **emissions** came not from burning living trees or **vegetation**, but from
 below ground.  

When we think fire, wildfires, we always  think as forest fire and the trees are burning and the emissions and pollutions
 are coming from the tree burning.  [**snorts**]

 Uh, but uh, surprisingly what we found was the carbon emissions came from the  the soil, the **peat**, and the ground materials.

 Canada is home to one quarter of the world's peat land, largely concentrated  in Ontario and Quebec. 

These boggy wetlands are made up of partly  decomposed plants that contain a lot of carbon stored underground for decades or
 even centuries. 

And when they catch fire, they burn slowly and deeply.

So, that means more polluting materials are being released uh, from the ground  because of a slow **smoldering** of the ground fire compared to the live tree fires.  

The fires can continue **smoldering**  underground for months, even through the winter, producing so-called **zombie** fires  that **reignite** in the spring. 

As a result of our **vast** **peat** land, Canada's  wildfires are thought to produce more greenhouse gas emissions than fires in  other countries.  

We're going to put a big **tariff** on  Canada because of the smoke.  And despite what the US president  claims, it also means preventing fires isn't as simple as better forest  management.  

The most effective way to deal with  these forest fires **in the long term** is to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from  other sectors and try to reduce global warming. 

Because as long as global  warming is getting worse, the fires will also continue to get worse.

Jeff Semple, Global News, Toronto.
